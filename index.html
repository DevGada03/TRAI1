Core Principles in Plain English

01

Why Standard AI Leaks Patient Data

Standard models easily memorize rare patients. If a patient has unusual blood test results, the AI learns their exact profile by heart, allowing a hacker to confirm whether that specific person was in the hospital's study.

02

The Noise Shield (Laplace Mechanism)

To protect identities, we calculate the maximum effect one patient can have on medical averages, then add a carefully measured amount of statistical random noise to mask individual entries.

03

Private Training & The Epsilon Budget

During training, we cap how much influence any single patient has and add protective noise. A live privacy fuel gauge (epsilon budget) automatically halts training before too much privacy is spent.

How Privacy Math Works

How Privacy Math Works: Sensitivity & Budget Allocation

A plain-English explanation of how much noise to inject into patient data so that individual identities are completely hidden while medical predictions stay accurate.

1. Sensitivity: How Much Can One Patient Change the Data?

Imagine two clinical datasets that are identical, except one person is added or removed. Sensitivity measures the maximum change this single person can cause to any medical average.

Sensitivity = (Maximum Possible Value − Minimum Possible Value) ÷ Total Patients (5,000)

Because there are 5,000 patients in the hospital dataset, one individual's effect on any test average is diluted down to less than 1/5000th of the possible range.

2. Adding Controlled Noise (Laplace Shield)

To prevent someone from figuring out who was in the dataset, we add a calculated amount of random statistical noise to each clinical test result.

Noise Amount = Feature Sensitivity ÷ Privacy Budget (ε)

Smaller ε (stricter privacy) → More noise is added to mask identities.
Larger ε (looser privacy) → Less noise is added, keeping numbers closer to the raw values.

3. Sharing the Privacy Budget Across 16 Patient Tests

Every time we look at a test (like blood pressure or glucose), we spend a portion of our total privacy budget. The total budget is the sum of what we spend on each test.

Total Privacy Budget Spent = Sum of Budgets Across All 16 Clinical Tests

Critical life-saving markers (such as Creatinine and Oxygen SpO₂) are given a slightly larger share of the budget so the model remains sharp at detecting heart and kidney failure.
